# "In place" means between the same two lines, not at the same time

A kept off-script region plays between the two lines it was spoken between. It cannot
play at the time it was said, and it does not try to.

## Context

The cut is assembled in script order, so a kept region has no original time to return to
— the material either side of it has moved. What it can keep is its neighbours, and it
follows the last **reading** heard before it: every take, not just the one that plays.

Two other anchors were tried and are wrong.

Using the **winning take** alone puts the mutter that introduces a retake *before* the
line it interrupted, because the reading that won is the one on the far side of it. The
mutter belongs after the attempt it abandoned, and only the full list of readings knows
that attempt happened.

Comparing by **where a line sits in the script** rather than by when a reading began
strands the region at the end of the cut whenever a session was recorded out of order —
which is exactly the case the script-order assembly exists to handle.

## Consequences

A kept region's neighbours in the cut are the ones it actually sat between when spoken.
That is what makes the `Off-script` marker's answer to "where do I look" true, and what
makes deleting the region the single keystroke the keep-and-mark asymmetry assumes it is.
